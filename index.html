The Lab

Most software built for African markets is adapted from products designed elsewhere. We work the other way.

Point
of
View

We spend time understanding how things already work — how schools collect fees, how landlords and tenants negotiate trust, how skilled tradespeople find clients, how creators get compensated. Then we build infrastructure around those realities, not against them.

The result is a portfolio of interconnected systems sharing a common payment and identity layer underneath. Each product starts as a research question. Each one is an answer we're still refining.

How we research

We study what already works before proposing what's new. Local behaviour is the design spec.

How we build

Ship to production early. Harden fast. Build for 2G. No assumptions about connectivity or device.

How we scale

Shared infrastructure across products. Solve mobile money once. Solve identity once. Then build everything on top.

How we measure

Transactions processed. Trust built. Problems that no longer need workarounds. Not vanity metrics.

Research

The questions
we're answering

Every product in the lab starts here. A problem observed in the field, sharpened into a question precise enough to build around.

01
How do schools serving millions of students manage fees, records, and official results without disrupting how they already work?
Cameroon's schools run on trust, discipline, and paper. The infrastructure needed to go digital had to fit inside that, not replace it.
Ubora Écoles
02
How does a skilled provider's reputation travel beyond the two neighborhoods they've already worked in?
In Douala, the best plumber is found by asking a neighbor. That system works — until the plumber wants to grow, or the neighbor isn't home.
Ezra Service
03
How does a tenant prove they pay on time when there's no credit bureau and no formal lease history?
Landlords in Cameroon rely on gut instinct because there's no data. Tenants who pay reliably for years have nothing to show for it when they move.
Ubora Living
04
How do creators in Cameroon get compensated without routing through platforms built for other markets?
Micro-influencers with real audiences are left out of brand deals because they can't receive international payouts. The audience is local. The infrastructure should be too.
Kreastar

On the Ground

The research
is working

We don't measure success in features shipped. We measure it in payments processed, landlords connected, and providers booked.

Écoles · Education

School fees collected digitally across Cameroon

Ubora Écoles processes tuition payments, tracks student records, and delivers official exam results — all from a single dashboard. Schools that used to manage everything on paper now have a full audit trail.

Living · PropTech

Rental payments tracked. Trust built.

Ubora Living is live in Douala. Landlords collect rent via Mobile Money, tenants get digital receipts, and every on-time payment builds a Rental Score — the first tenant reputation system of its kind in Cameroon.

EZRA · Services

Verified providers. On-demand, across Douala.

Ezra Service connects clients with vetted plumbers, electricians, and cleaners in Douala and Yaoundé. Providers are rated and paid securely — no cash, no chasing invoices.

Shared Infrastructure

The layer underneath
everything

The four products above share a common foundation. We built the payment and identity layers once — so every product built on top of them inherits the solution, not the problem.

UP
Ubora Pay

A unified payment API connecting MTN Mobile Money, Orange Money, and more. When operators update their APIs, only Ubora Pay updates — every product built on it stays working. We're opening this infrastructure to other builders in Cameroon. Integrate once, go live fast.

Open to builders · Live in production
ID
Ubora ID

A digital identity layer for the ecosystem. Powers KYC for Ubora Pay, ties Rental Scores to verified individuals in Living, anchors patient records in Health, verifies providers on Ezra. One identity. Every platform. Available as an API for any builder who needs it.
